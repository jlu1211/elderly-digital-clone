Elderly Digital Clone

An AI companion that calls an older parent every day and talks with them about their own life, using stories, photos and voice memories the family has approved, then sends the family a short summary.

The 30-second version

Older adults living alone are isolated, and their working children cannot be there every day. Families already pay for two things: memory books (Storyworth has printed over a million) and remote safety checks (Japanese families pay ¥1,000–¥3,300 a month to profitable companies like ALSOK and Zojirushi). No one sells a daily conversation grounded in the parent's own life. That is our product. The open question, which our interviews will test, is whether families pay monthly for conversation and not just for a safety check.

3 · The gap we fill

What exists

  • Memory products capture stories once and stop at the book.
  • Companions (ElliQ, Meela) talk daily but do not know the person's life.
  • Safety checks (Japan Post, Zojirushi) confirm the parent is all right but do not talk with them.
  • HereAfter AI, the closest, builds a voice avatar mainly for after a person dies.

What we build

  • A daily phone call, so the parent needs no new device or app.
  • Conversation grounded in family-approved memories (retrieval over stories, photos and voice notes).
  • A weekly summary to the family, with a flag if the parent misses calls or sounds unwell.
  • Business model: start as a memory gift, convert to a monthly plan, and pilot with one senior living community.

5 · What we still have to prove

  • Main risk Families pay for gifts and cheap safety checks; we have no evidence yet that they pay monthly for conversation. Amazon retired its $19.99/month Alexa Together in May 2025.
  • Older adults have to keep picking up the phone.
  • Using a relative's voice needs clear consent, and people with dementia may not be able to give it.
  • California's companion-chatbot law (SB 243, since January 2026) requires telling users it is AI and publishing self-harm protocols.
  • Inference cost per call has to stay below what families will pay; we will measure GPU-seconds per conversation.

Interview questions that decide it: What do you already spend each month on your parent's safety or care? Would you pay for a daily call that remembers your parent's stories plus a weekly summary, and at what price? Would your parent keep answering it?
